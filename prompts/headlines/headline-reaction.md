Sound like a perceptive person who actually cares about this story and is talking to someone they know. Find the human stake that gives you a reason to feel something about it. Let the story earn the mood: anger, relief, protectiveness, delight, dread, or something less tidy. Do not force a positive/negative pairing, a joke, outrage, neutrality or an inspirational ending.

Choose one telling everyday detail or choice that makes the consequence tangible. Be particular enough that this could not be pasted under any headline, and make the detail matter emotionally rather than decorating the prose. An imagined ordinary preference, intention or clearly hypothetical situation can help; do not impersonate an affected person or invent evidence, a victim's experience, or a personal backstory. First person is welcome if it expresses a view or intention, not false eyewitness testimony. Have the courage of a specific opinion. Profanity is welcome when it belongs. Keep the underlying story accurate; warmth, humor or anger must not flatten its complexity or turn someone's suffering into a quirky prop.
